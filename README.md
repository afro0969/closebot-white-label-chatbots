## "White label" means three different things

The phrase gets thrown around loosely. In practice, chatbot platforms white-label at one of three depths, and the price tag usually tracks the depth.

**Level 1 — logo on a chat widget.** You can swap out the chat bubble icon and color. The builder, the dashboard, the login screen, the onboarding emails, and sometimes the client-facing invoices still carry the vendor's name. This is the cheapest version and the one most often mislabeled as white label.

**Level 2 — branded builder and interface.** Your agency's domain, colors and logo appear in the tool your team works in, and often in the interface your client logs into. The vendor's brand is gone from the visible surfaces.

**Level 3 — branded client portal plus your own billing.** This is the one that actually produces recurring margin. The client logs in under your domain, sees your KPI dashboard, and pays you. You pay the platform. The markup is yours.

If a platform can't tell you clearly which of the three you're buying, that's your answer. A full white label means your client never sees the vendor's name anywhere — not in the tab title, not in the portal footer, not in the payment confirmation.

## Five questions that cut through most sales pages

Almost every demo looks the same for the first ten minutes. These questions separate platforms once a client's agent is actually live.

**How much control does the client get — and can they break it?** Give a business owner full access to the agent builder and some percentage of them will disable a qualifying step and quietly stop booking appointments for three weeks. CloseBot's V2 rebuild went the other direction deliberately: only the agency builds and edits agents, while the client fills in predefined variables and uploads knowledge base documents. The client personalizes without touching core logic.

**Who charges the client?** Some platforms let you rebill through their system with your own markup on messages, seats and storage. Others make you invoice separately and reconcile usage by hand.

**What happens when an AI provider goes down?** Model choice and automatic fallback matter more than they sound. If the platform only speaks to one provider's API, their outage is your outage.

**Does it fit the CRM your clients already use?** Native HighLevel and HubSpot integrations, custom CRM connections, or standalone operation — the answer decides how much glue work lands on your desk.

**How deep is the rebrand, technically?** Custom domain via your own DNS, your logo, your colors, your portal. If "white label" turns out to mean "customizable widget text," you'll find out during onboarding.
